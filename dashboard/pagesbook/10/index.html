Battle of the Fangs

A large pack of wolves ran quickly among the snowdrifts. At the front of the pack ran a big grey wolf. He was the leader of the pack and the strongest wolf among them. It was he who began to run faster and then looked at the she-wolf. He did not snarl at her when she tried to outrun him. On the contrary, he wanted to run near to her. But on her other side ran a strong, old wolf. His muzzle and body had the scar of many battles. He had only one eye. He always ran on her right side. At such times the old wolf did his best to touch her body, shoulder, or neck. But the she-wolf did not like this. She rejected his attention with her angry snarl and sharp teeth every time. The situation in the pack was desperate The lack of food and rising anger went together. The wolves were very weak and thin. But they ran as quickly as they could. At the back of the pack ran the old wolves, and at the front were the strongest ones. They ran many miles that day without stopping. They ran in a frozen and dead world. Nothing moved. They alone were alive, and they were looking for anything else that could survive the harsh conditions. Luckily they found a dead moose in the snow. They ate it at once. After that they slept for a long time with their full stomachs. The hunger was over. The next morning the pack ran down to the Mackenzie River. Each day one or two wolves died on the way. In the end, there were only three wolves left: the she-wolf, the one-eyed wolf, and the leader of the pack. The she-wolf was very angry at the time. Both the old wolf and young wolf had the marks of her teeth on them. They were kind to the she-wolf, but they were very angry at one another.

The Grey Cub

The grey cub was different from his brothers and sisters. They inherited the red colour of their fur from the she-wolf, He alone inherited the grey colour from his father. He looked like a real wolf. He knew his sisters and brothers very well. He played with them clumsily and slowly. He learned his mother by touch and smell. Most of the first month of his life he slept for a long time. But after a few months, he began to learn about his new world with great curiosity. Actually, his world was very small. Its limits were the walls of the cave. Still, he saw the bright light of the world outside. He always tried to reach this light by crawling toward the entrance of the cave. And his brothers and sisters followed his lead. Unfortunately, their mother always pushed them back from the entrance. He was an angry little cub. It was normal because he was a predator. His mother and father ate only meat. Yet he could snarl much louder than his brothers and sisters. It was he that first bit another cub by the ear angrily. And it was he that caused the mother the biggest trouble in the cave. The grey cub's desire for light grew from day to day. He always wanted to crawl towards the cave's entrance. Unfortunately, his mother pushed him back every time. The grey cub was growing up quickly. Every day he went out of the cave and discovered a new area near the stream. He learned how to hunt his little prey and stay unharmed. As it happened the hunger came again. The she-wolf spent all day in search of meat. The grey cub himself tried to hunt some squirrels and mice. Soon the hunger was over. Chapter 4

The Bondage

Every day brought something new for White Fang. He ran about the camp, searching, discovering, learning. He quickly learned about the man-animals and their rules. The more he came to know them, the more they showed their god-like power. White Fang believed in these gods and their power easily.
